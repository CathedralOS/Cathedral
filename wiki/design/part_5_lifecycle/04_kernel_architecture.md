# Chapter 04: Kernel Architecture

> The decision of what the privileged core actually is: the smallest trusted substrate that can carry Cathedral's component, capability, and update story.

## The Legacy Model

Monolithic kernels fuse everything privileged — drivers, filesystems, network stacks, schedulers — into one address space where any bug is a total compromise. Microkernels split that out into servers but pay in IPC overhead and complexity, and in practice still grow a fat trusted base. Hybrids inherit both sets of problems. Across all of them the privileged boundary is drawn by *hardware rings and address spaces*, and the question "what must actually be trusted, and what can crash or be replaced without killing the system?" is answered by accident of history rather than by design.

## The Cathedral Model

Pick the **smallest privileged substrate** that supports the component, capability, and update story — not the cleanest-sounding diagram. The architecture is not chosen by aesthetics ("microkernel sounds clean"); it is chosen by answering, for each piece of the system, four questions: **what must be trusted? what can be restarted? what can be upgraded? what can be proven? — and what can crash without killing the system?** The answer follows from Omega's isolation guarantees, not from x86 ring boundaries.

The boundary is **OS code versus app code.** The OS is written in Omega and is proof-carrying, so its own components isolate from one another by the type system rather than the MMU, which is what makes a Theseus-style single address space and frictionless hot swap ([[updates_and_hot_swap]]) possible *for the OS*. App code is the other side: a user app can be written in anything, C++ included, so the OS cannot trust its instruction stream and isolates it with hardware (separate address space, MMU, instruction trapping) while the capability model confines its system access. Language isolation is the OS's internal mechanism; hardware isolation is the wall between the OS and untrusted apps. That is the normal case, not just legacy boxes, and where exactly the wall sits is *the* architectural decision.

The **core itself** is the one thing on neither side of that wall — it is *trusted, not contained*, the exact inverse of a driver (which is *contained, not trusted*). You cannot cage the core, because the core is what *builds* the cages: it programs the IOMMU, the MMIO maps, and the device resets, so there is nothing above it to confine it. Its assurance comes from proof + TCB-minimization, not a wall. The whole system therefore reduces to a clean dichotomy — every part is either **caged** (untrusted, hardware-confined: apps, drivers) or **proved** (the trusted core, kept as small as possible) — with only a thin, hand-audited hardware seam left trusted-and-unproven. This also fixes the test for what can ever be *required*: only what the cage (hardware + core) enforces over a component's head; anything asked *of* an untrusted component is best-effort, safe to rely on only with a hardware backstop.

## Concerns & Design Space

- **The important axis.** Trust / restartability / upgradability / provability / crash-isolation, evaluated per subsystem — drivers, FS, net, scheduler — rather than one global verdict.
- **Single address space + language isolation.** Zero-copy IPC and frictionless hot swap, conditioned on proved Omega; the boundary-provider set becomes much of the TCB ([[omega_substrate]]).
- **SAS safety = proof *or* a capability-ISA; the cheap call and the proof-dependence are one coin.** A single address space buys near-zero "syscalls" — a call into an OS service is a plain **function call**, no trap, no ring switch, no TLB flush (Singularity measured ~100× over a traditional syscall) — *precisely because* there is no MMU boundary to cross. That is exactly why memory-safety *inside* the SAS must come from elsewhere: **either PCC validating pointer accesses (software proof) or a CHERI-style capability-ISA** (unforgeable hardware bounds the CPU checks on every dereference — real; ARM Morello, 2022). **CHERI gives *both* — shared-address-space cheap calls *and* hardware-enforced isolation with no PCC** — so a capability-ISA is what turns the whole performance story from a bet into a hardware guarantee; on commodity hardware without it, the SAS rests on the proof. Crucially the proof-dependence is **localized**: app↔app and app↔OS isolation is the **MMU** (apps are hardware-walled, below), so a checker/PCC failure can never leak one app's memory to another — it could only dent the core's *internal* isolation. So the proof is a **performance bet over an MMU-safe baseline, scoped to the OS core**: if it fails, cross-core-component calls fall back to IPC cost (a slower capability microkernel), never a safety break.
- **The accumulating case against SAS-via-PCC — lean toward MMU→CHERI as the plausible real-world path.** The SAS-on-software-proof bet keeps adding costs as chapters mature: recompile-and-hot-swap for in-place changes, proof-aware foreign boundaries, and **debugging** — raw byte-patching (`int3`, trampolines) cannot be injected into the SAS without breaking its sole invariant (only checked code is admitted), so SAS-native instrumentation must be *checker-verified* or the component *temporarily walled* ([[debugging_and_tracing]]). Scheduler fairness is not one of those proof costs: Cathedral's timer may architecturally preempt native code at any instruction; only structured cancellation/migration latency depends on explicit semantic safe points ([[scheduler_and_resources]]). Compounding the remaining costs: **PCC for a *serious systems language* has been done essentially zero times in history** — Necula-style PCC proved narrow type-safety on small programs; CompCert / seL4 are whole-program *expert* verification, not modular proof that travels with arbitrary shipped code — so SAS-via-PCC bets on inventing the unprecedented. **CHERI is the more grounded direction:** it gives memory-safety *and* compartment crossings cheaper than the MMU *in hardware, with no PCC* (Morello shipped 2022; CHERIoT for embedded). It is not free — a compartment switch costs more than the ideal SAS's plain call, and it bets on capability silicon reaching the target platform — but that is a *hardware-availability* bet on something real, not a *research* bet on something unprecedented. So the honest lean: treat **MMU-by-default + CHERI-where-available** as the plausible shipping architecture, and **SAS-via-PCC as the aspirational ceiling** — kept because the MMU fallback is always correct (the whole point of the localized-bet framing), not because the proof is expected to land first.
- **Shrinking the compiler-in-TCB.** Because SAS isolation rests on proof, the *compiler* is in the TCB — a miscompile breaks every boundary at once, with no MMU backstop. The answer is the same shrink-and-bottom-out move used everywhere else: a hand-audited bootstrap seed (so no Thompson *trusting-trust* binary can hide in the lineage), a small proof-checking kernel that re-checks compiled artifacts, and a verified Omega→machine translation that the kernel checks (CompCert / CakeML-style). Trust then bottoms out at a tiny, legible **{seed, checker, specs, hardware}** — never zero (Gödel: a system cannot prove its own foundation, and a proof only shows code meets its *spec*, never that the spec is the right one) but small enough to read by hand. This becomes canon once Omega self-hosts; until then the Rust-implemented compiler leans on the differential oracle as the interim mitigation ([../../../../Omega/wiki/cathedral_alignment.md](../../../../Omega/wiki/cathedral_alignment.md)).
- **The trust base sharpened: one first-party checker, the spec is the soft underbelly, and proof is a gradient.** Of the `{seed, checker, specs, hardware}` base, two entries deserve first-class treatment. **The checker is Omega's own** (the bootstrap-lattice's proof-checker — *no third-party tooling*) and is the **single** trust anchor that validates everything by re-checking: compiled-code certs (Thompson-resistance), verification certs (inductive proofs), and library proofs (a native `Proofs.lock`-style construct, Cargo-to-Rust). Auditing that one tiny verified-to-machine-code checker is the irreducible human cost — accepted (`cake_lpr`, a SAT-checker verified to machine code, is the existence proof it is practical). **The spec is the soft, load-bearing entry:** a proof shows code-meets-spec, *never* spec-is-the-right-spec, and that gap is **unsolvable** (intent is not formalizable), so the discipline is to keep specs **small, legible, and tested** — to give the only thing that touches the gap, *human review*, its best shot — and to **never sell "proven" as "correct"** (certification reads "proven-against-spec-S," not "correct"). This is empirically where failures cluster: ≈96% of real zero-knowledge soundness bugs are spec/encoding errors, not proof-checking errors. And **verification is a gradient, not all-or-nothing:** every component rides the *free* floor (memory-safety, always; bounds/totality, cheap), while deep functional proofs are an *opt-in* tier reserved for load-bearing code — so "designed-to-be-verified" means *can-be* + *the critical parts are*, **not** *everything is proven*. (Safety is proven unconditionally; *liveness* rides the trusted scheduler's fairness, [[scheduler_and_resources]]. The real cost is proof *authoring*, not checking — the LLM-authoring bet, Omega `totality_and_bounded_computation.md`.)
- **Side channels: parity, not a SAS penalty — plus a constant-time edge.** SAS does *not* worsen microarchitectural leakage: the MMU was never a side-channel defense (Spectre/Meltdown cross it anyway), SAS drops it only between mutually-trusting core components, and untrusted code stays hardware-walled and *unmapped* from the core — so exposure ≈ any OS's, arguably better on the Meltdown vector. Proof gives no edge here (it is architectural; side channels are microarchitectural). Default: baseline hardware mitigations everywhere + constant-time crypto. Beyond that, a domain carries a declared **`IsolationClass`** — a total-order ladder `baseline → flush → partition → exclusive` (an OS-defined `data` value, set on the component or raised per-span by an ownership-scoped guard, gated at spawn by capability and metered). The **core** enforces it ([[scheduler_and_resources]]): *temporal* leaks via a flush at the context switch (keyed to the pair — exit-flush the outgoing hardened domain's cache, entry-flush predictors before an incoming one), *spatial* leaks via placement (the unit is the physical core, whose SMT threads share L1 + predictors — `exclusive` owns the core, `partition` co-locates only trusted work + partitions L3). "Require = cage": the core flushes/places; the component only declares.
- **Hardware isolation for app code — and for drivers.** Any code the Omega toolchain did not compile and prove (most user apps, in any language) is untrusted at the instruction level and isolated by MMU/address space and instruction trapping, with capabilities confining its system access ([[security_policy_and_sandboxing]]). Drivers are the same case, deliberately: they run as confined user-mode components, *contained not trusted*, so a bad driver corrupts only its own device ([[driver_model]]). Because device DMA bypasses the CPU's MMU, containing a device needs an **IOMMU** — Cathedral mandates one as the hardware floor. (A stricter **LLM-era policy option**: refuse foreign binaries entirely — *only checkable code runs* — which makes the hardware wall **optional defense-in-depth** rather than a load-bearing foreign-code mechanism, and dissolves the compat-box/SFI complexity. Viable because LLMs make producing a compliant binary cheap, and Omega gains adoption before any compliant foreign-language compiler ships; the "only-checkable-runs" constraint becomes a coherence/security feature, at the cost of a smaller *initial* ecosystem.)
- **What stays privileged.** The minimal substrate likely owns only: capability enforcement; extent/address-space management including IOMMU mappings and MMIO-view minting; installation of immutable admitted executable artifacts; scheduling primitives; interrupt-entry installation and the external-root ledger; provider admission/selection; and the hardware root-of-trust handoff. There is no general executable-memory/JIT authority. Checked assembly implements the hardware seam, while plan/capability contracts keep each operation attributable ([[hardware_foundation_profile]], [[driver_model]], [[boot_and_trust_chain]]).
- **Code injection and control-flow integrity are separate concerns.** The admitted-artifact loader prevents ordinary bytes from becoming host code. Checked-Omega backward-edge returns derive from memory safety and compiler-owned, non-addressable live stack/control state; WCSU supplies fixed stack-capacity evidence. Forward-edge indirect calls and tail calls still require sealed requirement-compatible entry references or descriptors, while admitted opaque providers must preserve their call/state exits or remain isolated. Independent final-byte transfer validation and CET/PAC/shadow-stack hardening are later PCC/TCB assurance, not an Omega source-language or timer prerequisite ([[hardware_foundation_profile]]).
- **Restartable subsystems.** Everything not in the minimal core should be a restartable, upgradable component, including most of what monolithic kernels keep privileged.
- **Minimal trusted broker = route tickets, never hold authority.** Unavoidable middlemen (network demux, observability/audit query, service-activator routing) stay TCB-worthy by *routing capability tickets they cannot themselves redeem* — a `{slot, generation}` ticket only redeems in the *target's* arena, so the broker holds no authority and revocation stays the target's generation bump. The discipline per broker: name the one irreducible trusted invariant and prove only that (demux: "this flow → the right inbox, never cross-delivered"); keep *classification/routing* trusted but push *processing* to untrusted endpoints (the whole TCP stack lives in each world); inspect headers, never payload — **seal** payloads so the broker sits structurally outside the confidentiality TCB; never let trusted routing depend on the untrusted driver; and lean on hardware steering (per-world NIC queues / SR-IOV) so the software broker shrinks toward zero. Smell test: a broker that *looks up* "am I allowed?" holds power (wrong); one that is *handed* what to route does not — if it needs a standing capability, a primitive is missing from the core, so add it rather than fatten the broker.
- **Options on the table.** Monolithic, microkernel, hybrid, exokernel-ish, library-OS-ish, Theseus-style SAS, capability kernel, hypervisor-first — held as candidates, not commitments, until the target hardware and workload constrain them.

## Key Questions

- What is the irreducible privileged core — the set whose compromise is fatal — and how small can it be made?
- Where is the line drawn between language-isolated OS code (proved Omega, single address space) and hardware-isolated app code (untrusted, any language)?
- How much of the classic kernel can become restartable components without losing the performance the target workload needs?
- Is a hypervisor-first base worth it for running a legacy box ([[compatibility_and_legacy]]) safely alongside the native system?

## Omega Leverage

- **Language-level isolation** is the substrate's premise: ownership, borrowing, and effect/authority checking provide isolation that the MMU otherwise provides, per [../../../../Omega/wiki/language_guide/chapter_19_capabilities_effects_boundaries.md](../../../../Omega/wiki/language_guide/chapter_19_capabilities_effects_boundaries.md).
- **The boundary-provider set is much of the TCB.** Omega's boundary registry makes the trusted base *enumerable* — the registered providers are precisely what must be trusted, and the build report lists them.
- **`reaches` + authority-flow ceilings** let even privileged components be bounded and audited, so "privileged" is not synonymous with "unbounded."
- **The hardware foundation** separates inert addresses, extent authority,
  layout/access plans, checked instructions, entry roots, and external loans, so
  low-level code does not regain C's "number equals power" escape
  ([[hardware_foundation_profile]]).
- **Replaceable provider realizations** ([../../../../Omega/wiki/language_guide/chapter_22_versioned_data.md](../../../../Omega/wiki/language_guide/chapter_22_versioned_data.md))
  make core subsystems upgradable where their owned closure, resource provision,
  entry accounting, and state-disposition policy can be validated. A package or
  arbitrary machine is not a swap point merely by existing.

## Open Questions

- Miscompilation is answered by the TCB-minimization strategy above, and side channels by the `IsolationClass` posture (parity + opt-in flush/placement); the residue *inside the proved core* is the irreducible {seed, checker, specs, hardware} base — small and human-audited, never zero — including the one hardware fact constant-time rests on (the CPU's data-independent-timing guarantee, ARM DIT / Intel DOITM). (Drivers are excluded — hardware-confined, not language-trusted, [[driver_model]].)
- Performance is now framed honestly: the SAS win is real *inside the proved core* (IPC-heavy / service-mesh / OS-service workloads — possibly multiples), Linux-class at the untrusted-app seam, and parity-or-slower for compute-bound work *until* the optimizer matures — which Omega's higher-ceiling + verified-LLM-optimization loop should accelerate ([../../../../Omega/wiki/design_briefs/verified_gated_ml_optimizer.md](../../../../Omega/wiki/design_briefs/verified_gated_ml_optimizer.md)). The residual open part is the empirical crossover — how much of a real desktop workload is seam-dominated.
- Does the architecture decision even resolve before the target platform is fixed, or is it deliberately deferred ([[vision_and_non_goals]])?
