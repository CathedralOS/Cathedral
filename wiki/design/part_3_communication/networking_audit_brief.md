> Status: working output of a multi-agent audit/synthesis, kept as an artifact to deep-dive from. Nothing here is a decided design. The spine is small; most of this is a map of what still needs crunching. Do not treat as a chapter. Move a topic into `00_ipc_and_service_invocation.md`, `02_distributed_boundary.md`, or the networking chapter only after it is agreed in conversation.

## The spine (already implied by existing chapters)

- **Flow-as-capability.** A network capability is a flow authorization: the right to exchange packets with a named, identity-pinned peer, enforced at the shared NIC. Not "can network," not a socket. Default-deny is the absence of a grant.
- **Ports deleted locally, demoted on the wire.** No listening integer inside the machine; a service is its endpoint capability, and "bind" means register an endpoint. This kills `EADDRINUSE`, privileged `<1024`, `SO_REUSEPORT` theft, and ephemeral exhaustion. A port's three fused jobs split: demux goes to broker steering, identity to the pinned peer key, authority to the held flow capability. Forced: the wire still carries a 16-bit port, and a public server must listen on 443/53/22 and demux after accept.
- **Send and receive are two capabilities; the session is bidirectional.** Egress is `Reach<Peer>`; ingress is `Serve` (reachable-as-key). They are independently grantable and revocable. Once established a flow is bidirectional, like local IPC.
- **Per-Matrix `127.0.0.1`.** Loopback resolves inside the Matrix's own resolution environment. The Matrix asks for its own loopback grant, with no rethrow to the outer Matrix. The localhost-daemon disaster class is uninstantiable for native code.
- **Demux's four-line contract.** It does exactly four things: (1) admission, dropping non-authorized peers at flow-install; (2) cross-delivery prevention, the one provable invariant, so a packet lands in the right world's inbox; (3) flow-table arbitration over finite hardware steering slots, with overflow to software; (4) egress pacing and fair-queueing. It MUST NOT parse or terminate transport, hold redeemable standing authority (routes are handed tickets), see plaintext, or trust the NIC driver for routing. It routes on an endpoint-supplied routing token (flow-handle, QUIC Connection ID, or 5-tuple), never on semantic state.
- **Transport lives in each world.** TCP, QUIC, TLS, congestion control, and NAT traversal are a userspace library, in the exokernel/Demikernel direction. Congestion fairness is the scheduler's job: per-world paced `EgressBudget`, fair-queued.

## The hard residuals (the crunch list)

1. **First-pin / cold-stranger trust.** The keystone. Crypto is solved; the human cold-trust bootstrap is not. The dominant attack is non-cryptographic: a transparency-logged confusable lookalike. Needs confusable/mixed-script hard-blocks against the user's own pins, refuse-secret-surface-from-low-pin, born-low provenance-gated ceilings, a k-of-n OS-vendor root, and a store-introducer quorum. Parked as needing security expertise; see `../speculation/network_trust_fabric.md`.
2. **Relay economics and the commons.** Inbound behind CGNAT requires a relay. Relay demand concentrates on the exact battery/CGNAT devices the commons asks not to serve, so the vendor-seeded relay floor is a permanent recurring cost and a centralization/censorship chokepoint, not a cold-start crutch. "No central servers" holds for integrity, not for availability behind CGNAT.
3. **Legacy public-ingress front-end.** In the chapter under "Legacy public ingress: the exposed front-end". A carved-out minimal front-end (not the core demux) accepts unauthenticated TCP/TLS, reads the ClientHello SNI, and steers to the serving world (recursive inbound-nests). It is SNI-passthrough and never terminates TLS; under ECH it holds only the scoped ECH key. Dead by default, so a non-hosting machine has zero stranger-facing open ports; the front-end exists only under a `Serve<Public>` grant, distinct from and higher-authority than `Serve<Key>`. DoS is inherent (SYN floods like nginx; standard mitigations). Cathedral's contribution is isolation (compromise contained to its Matrix) and attribution (every accept steered into a named world), not elimination.
4. **Demux token discipline under QUIC.** In the chapter under "QUIC routing and the classifier discipline". The broker mints and partitions the CID space (QUIC-LB). Route on the token, never on semantics. The cross-delivery invariant scopes to broker-minted CIDs, with 5-tuple fallback for stock peers. A protocol-agnostic always-on router core plus an on-demand proved classifier module loaded into the router domain at hot-path speed (proof-isolation, no per-packet IPC), hot-swappable. `RegisterClassifier` (OS-only TCB) is split from `Serve` (app). Trap-upward is provider-IPC up the Matrix tree to the OS broker: one driver, one broker, N provider-endpoints, with SR-IOV as optional performance.
5. **The open-web client tier.** In the chapter under "The open-web client: contain, don't enumerate". URL-bar-as-mint is out: the OS does not own the browser's chrome, so a self-reported navigation is an untrusted claim, not a mint. The client is just a Matrix with full net and zero ambient local authority, so broad reach is harmless because the sandbox is empty of anything to steal (the conjunction ceiling, `Network` reach ∧ no `Read<sensitive>`, in canonical form). The picker is the only inbound bridge. Per-trust-domain browser instances (throwaway versus banking) are the cheap isolation win. Residual: it can still leak what you actively put into it this session (typed passwords, picked files); the blast radius is bounded to what this instance was handed, not to zero. The POSIX shim (connect maps to authorize) and the developer-Matrix for the inner loop still apply.
6. **"Locally-terminated / synthetic" fact versus Matrix indistinguishability.** A positive "this flow never left the machine" indicator is meaningful only to a child that brought its own pinned anchor. For confined code, "are you really online" must stay unanswerable by construction. Do not promise a trustworthy offline indicator.
7. **Finite steering-slot allocation.** In the chapter under "Hardware steering slots as a scheduled capability". The capability is the right to be scheduled into the finite hardware fast-path pool, not slot ownership. The broker places and evicts opportunistically, fair-shared via the scheduler, with overflow to the software path. Allocation status is not exposed, which closes the direct channel. The residual is a timing channel (own-latency reveals fast-versus-software), irreducible but low-SNR: a microsecond demux delta against millisecond network jitter is negligible for real flows. Two tiers: opaque dynamic scheduling (default, maximum utilization) versus a partitioned/reserved sub-pool (paranoid or local, closes even timing, costs idle entries).
8. **Recovery without a backdoor**, plus per-relationship-key cross-device continuity: unlinkable by default, linkable only by the user's own intent expressed through their authority graph.

## Ceilings (cannot be engineered away)

- **Tunnel-over-authorized-flow exfil is not closeable.** Capabilities bound the peer set, not the bytes. This is the permanent ceiling of the whole capability model; only conjunction ceilings (for example, deny `Read<Photo>` plus `Network` reach) structurally help.
- **Metadata always leaks.** Pinning hides content and identity, never existence-of-flow, timing, or the social graph.
- **Per-peer egress is unenforceable at the legacy CDN edge.** One IP fronts ~50k tenants and ECH hides the SNI, so the grant authorizes the whole shared front.
- **Inbound reachability is not the local OS's to solve.** The public mapping lives in upstream middleboxes (CGNAT/ISP). The OS can run ICE, prefer IPv6, and relay, but cannot conjure a public address.
- **The headline native wins scale with the Cathedral-native and store-mediated fraction of a user's peers**, which is near zero at launch. Those wins are mobility, no port configuration, and no phoning-home. Day-one value is against the legacy internet: no ambient exfil, per-app egress attenuation, one observable authority graph, and QUIC mobility.

## p2p / future, grounded (commit-now vs speculative)

- **Commit now:** bind a flow to a self-certifying peer key (onion-v3, libp2p PeerID, npub); carry identity as a QUIC Connection ID (mobility by default). WireGuard's `peer-key→allowed-IPs` is the coarse prior art that flow-as-capability generalizes. A Cathedral network provider is Tailscale/Nebula: resolve, authorize, relay.
- **Urbit:** take key-as-identity and the scry read-only namespace; reject scarce saleable address space, the bespoke VM, and the sponsorship hierarchy.
- **Speculative (keep parked):** UDP/QUIC is not universally passable (TCP fallback re-inherits the 4-tuple); `name→key` for a stranger still needs consensus; `address=pubkey` routing (Yggdrasil/cjdns) does not scale to internet size.

## Capability-purity flags to resolve

- **Contribution commons.** "Default-on by being a device" is role-blessed authority. Make it a held, visible, revocable `Commons::Serve(budget)`.
- **Rendezvous DHT.** This is the one flat global namespace Cathedral otherwise avoids. Quarantine it as a location hint only, never identity or authority.
- **Root resolver.** It must be a held `Resolve<namespace>` capability, not an ambient "resolve any name" service.
